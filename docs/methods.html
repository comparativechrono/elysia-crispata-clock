Clock genes

1. Reference proteins and candidates

102 reference clock proteins (human, mouse, Drosophila, monarch, zebrafish, oyster, Aplysia, squid, scallop) were fetched from UniProt and searched with DIAMOND (very-sensitive) against the Ensembl proteins of xgElyCris1.1, the Purdue-assembly proteins and 16 comparison proteomes. Scripts 01 to 03.

2. Orthology

Each family was aligned with MAFFT, trimmed with trimAl and a tree built with FastTree (LG + gamma, SH-like supports). A gene is named when it falls in the clade of its reference proteins and is the reciprocal best hit of the human or fly gene. Opsins were additionally required to have the retinal-binding lysine. Scripts 04, 05, 09.

3. Choosing a protein model

For each gene the candidates were the Ensembl model, the Purdue model, and translations of the genome guided by orthologues from other sea slugs, Aplysia and Biomphalaria (miniprot). Annotated models that do not re-align cleanly to their own assembly were set aside. Candidates were scored by mean bit score against orthologues in four outgroup molluscs; an existing annotation was kept unless a guided translation beat it by 5% and 30 bits. Six genes ended up with guided translations. Script 06.

4. Presence in each assembly

The chosen protein was aligned to all four assemblies with miniprot. Intact: at least 97% of the protein in one locus at 95% identity or more, with no frameshift or stop. Whole, sequence differs: the same with a frameshift or stop. In pieces: at least 90% found but split across loci. Partial: less than that. Missing: no locus at 95% identity. Scripts 07, 08.

5. Reads and RNA

Exons of every gene (from the Purdue models, with 150 bp flanks, repeat-containing parts masked) were included as baits when all HiFi reads were screened; a read counts when it aligns over 150 bp at 95% or better. Head RNA-seq was quantified with salmon against the Purdue coding sequences (37.5% of pairs assigned). Scripts 16, 20.

Kleptoplasts and gene transfer

6. Screening every read

All reads were mapped (minimap2, k = 15) to a panel of 241 green-algal and 7 other plastid genomes, 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 1,249 nuclear rDNA sequences of Bryopsidales, the four bacterial genomes, and the slug mitochondrion and rDNA. Fourteen low-complexity windows that attracted thousands of unrelated reads were masked. Scripts 10, 11.

7. Naming the algae

Plastid reads were assigned to their best-matching plastid genome (divergence 3% or less over 1 kb or more). rbcL and tufA were cut out of every read spanning them, identical sequences counted, and each haplotype compared with 6,453 GenBank barcodes of Bryopsidales. Script 13.

8. Chimeric reads

Reads with a panel match and 500 bp or more left unexplained were re-aligned base by base (minimap2 map-hifi) to the Sanger primary and alternate assemblies plus the panel, and the remainder to the Purdue and Tampa assemblies. A junction needs a slug segment next to a non-slug segment, each at least 300 bp at 97% identity or better. Read totals for each class use confident matches only (3% divergence or less over 1 kb or more). Two reads count as independent molecules only if both of their ends differ by more than 50 bp. Scripts 14, 15.

9. Chloroplast DNA in the assemblies

All four assemblies were cut into 10 kb windows and aligned base by base to the same panel. Matches of 200 bp or more at 97% identity or better are the exact copies. The rest were filtered for simple repeats (trinucleotide entropy of the matched panel sequence below 4.5 bits, or a panel window hit by 15 or more assembly windows) and whatever remained at 80% identity or better was listed one by one. For each exact plastid copy, a window of the copy plus 8 kb of neighbouring slug DNA was cut out. Repeats in the slug part were found empirically (100 bp bins hit by 4 or more of 20,000 reads) and masked, and every read of two of the three runs (ERR12356115 and ERR12353011, 53% of the data) was mapped to the masked windows; reads landing within 1.5 kb of a boundary were then aligned base by base. A read carries the junction if one alignment spans it by 300 bp on each side. It contradicts the junction if it matches 1 kb or more of slug flank at 98% or better, stops within 50 bp of the boundary, and has 300 bp or more left over. Scripts 22 to 27.

10. Algal genes by protein similarity

Every annotated protein (longest isoform per Ensembl gene, and all Purdue proteins) was searched with DIAMOND (sensitive) against the animal, algal, bacterial and fungal proteins listed on the gene-transfer page. A protein is listed when its best match overall is not an animal, and called a strong candidate when that match beats the best non-sacoglossan animal match by 20% in bit score. Separately, 471 plastid and photosynthesis proteins were aligned to all four assemblies with miniprot; overlapping hits were merged into loci, and the translation of each locus was searched against the same database to tell an algal gene from the slug’s own homologue. Scripts 21, 28, 29.

Caveats

  • Gene roles are inferred from homology with flies and mammals. Nothing here measures a rhythm.
  • xgElyCris1.1 is incomplete (BUSCO 73.6% complete; about 60% of read k-mers represented). A gene missing from it is not missing from the animal, as the catalogue shows.
  • Six protein models are translations guided by another species’ protein and may miss lineage-specific exons. The gene pages say which.
  • Tree supports are FastTree local supports, not bootstraps. Where a clade is weakly supported the call rests on reciprocal best hits.
  • Expression values come from one head sample at one unknown time of day.
  • Independent work; not reviewed by the genome authors.
