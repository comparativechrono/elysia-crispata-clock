Methods and data for Elysia crispata

All results for E. crispata were computed from publicly available data. The scripts are in the repository, and the numbered steps below follow the order in which they are run.

Clock genes

1. Reference proteins and candidates

A set of 102 reference clock proteins (from human, mouse, Drosophila, monarch butterfly, zebrafish, oyster, Aplysia, squid and scallop) was retrieved from UniProt and searched with DIAMOND (very-sensitive mode) against the Ensembl proteins of xgElyCris1.1, the proteins of the Purdue assembly and 16 comparison proteomes (scripts 01 to 03).

2. Orthology

Each gene family was aligned with MAFFT and trimmed with trimAl, and a tree was built with FastTree (LG model with gamma-distributed rates; SH-like support values). A gene was assigned when it fell within the clade of its reference proteins and was the reciprocal best hit of the human or Drosophila gene. Opsins were also required to have the retinal-binding lysine (scripts 04, 05 and 09).

3. Selection of a protein model

For each gene, the candidate models were the Ensembl model, the Purdue model and homology-guided translations of the genome based on orthologues from other sea slugs, Aplysia and Biomphalaria (miniprot). Annotated models that could not be re-aligned to their own assembly without discrepancies were excluded. Candidates were scored by their mean bit score against the orthologues of four other molluscs, and an existing annotation was retained unless a homology-guided translation scored at least 5% and 30 bits higher. Homology-guided translations were selected for six genes (script 06).

4. Status in each assembly

The selected protein was aligned to all four assemblies with miniprot. A gene was classed as intact when at least 97% of the protein aligned at a single locus with at least 95% identity and without a frameshift or stop codon; as complete with a frameshift or stop codon when the same criteria were met apart from a frameshift or stop codon; as fragmented when at least 90% of the protein was found but split between loci; as partial when less than 90% was found; and as not found when no locus aligned with at least 95% identity (scripts 07 and 08).

5. Read support and expression

The exons of every gene (from the Purdue gene models, with 150 bp of flanking sequence and with repeat-containing regions masked) were included as baits when the HiFi reads were screened. A read was counted when it aligned over at least 150 bp with at least 95% identity. Expression in the head was quantified from the RNA-seq library with salmon, using the Purdue coding sequences as the reference (37.5% of read pairs were assigned; scripts 16 and 20).

Kleptoplasts and gene transfer

6. Screening of all reads

All reads were mapped with minimap2 (k = 15) to a panel consisting of 241 green-algal and seven other plastid genomes, 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 1,249 nuclear rDNA sequences of Bryopsidales, the four bacterial genomes, and the mitochondrial genome and rDNA of the slug. Fourteen low-complexity windows that attracted thousands of unrelated reads were masked (scripts 10 and 11).

7. Identification of the algae

Plastid reads were assigned to the plastid genome that they match best (with at most 3% divergence over at least 1 kb). The rbcL and tufA sequences were extracted from every read that spans them, identical sequences were counted, and each haplotype was compared with 6,453 GenBank barcode sequences of Bryopsidales (script 13).

8. Chimeric reads

Reads with a match to the panel and at least 500 bp not covered by that match were re-aligned at base level (minimap2, map-hifi preset) to the primary and alternate assemblies of xgElyCris1.1 together with the panel, and reads that remained unexplained were aligned to the Purdue and Tampa assemblies. A junction was defined as a nuclear segment adjacent to a non-nuclear segment, each at least 300 bp long with at least 97% identity. The total number of reads in each class was based on confident matches only (at most 3% divergence over at least 1 kb). Two reads were counted as independent molecules only if both of their ends differed by more than 50 bp. Reads that had been set aside because they matched a plastid genome only weakly were searched in the same alignments for plastid segments of 200 bp or more that are not simple repeats (scripts 14, 15 and 17).

Two steps of the pipeline that was developed later for the other species were also applied to this library (scripts/genus/run_screens.sh, step crispata). First, reads in which the first pass had found a plastid or mitochondrial segment of 60 to 5,000 bases with at least 95% identity, with at least 200 bases of the read on each side, and not in a panel region matched by more than six such reads (slug repeats that resemble that region), were retrieved again from the archive. They were aligned at base level to the primary assembly and the panel, and searched for an organelle segment of 100 bases or more lying within a single continuous stretch of nuclear DNA, either between two alignments or as an insertion within one alignment. Second, 40 evenly spaced blocks from each run, amounting to one read in a hundred, were aligned in the same way, in order to count how often a nuclear read is joined to nuclear DNA from a different assembly sequence or from more than 50 kb away, and how many reads are duplicates of another read (reads that share both ends). Scripts genus/54, 52, 31d, 33 and 46.

Finally, every read containing a junction between chloroplast DNA and nuclear DNA was retrieved again, and the junctions were compared with each other directly: the 400 bases on either side of each junction were aligned between all pairs of reads. Two reads were considered to share a junction when the alignment extended across it for at least 100 bases on both sides, placed the junction of one read within 6 bases of the junction of the other, contained no gap of that size next to it, and was at least 98% identical on both sides. Reads that share an end were treated as duplicates of a single molecule. This approach detects junctions in repetitive nuclear DNA, which cannot be found by comparing positions, and junctions for which the two reads had been placed on different assemblies. Scripts genus/52, 33, 46 and 51.

9. Chloroplast DNA in the assemblies

All four assemblies were divided into 10 kb windows and aligned at base level to the same panel. Matches of 200 bp or more with at least 97% identity were classed as exact copies. The remaining matches were filtered for simple repeats (trinucleotide entropy of the matched panel sequence below 4.5 bits, or a panel window hit by 15 or more assembly windows), and all those remaining with at least 80% identity were listed individually.

For each exact plastid copy, a window comprising the copy and 8 kb of adjacent nuclear DNA was extracted. Repeats in the nuclear part were identified empirically (100 bp bins hit by four or more of 20,000 reads) and masked, and all reads from two of the three runs (ERR12356115 and ERR12353011, 53% of the data) were mapped to the masked windows. Reads that mapped within 1.5 kb of a boundary were then aligned at base level. A read was counted as containing the junction if a single alignment spanned it by at least 300 bp on each side. A read was counted as contradicting the junction if it matched at least 1 kb of the nuclear flank with at least 98% identity, its alignment ended within 50 bp of the boundary, and at least 300 bp of the read remained unaligned (scripts 22 to 27).

10. Search for algal genes by protein similarity

Every annotated protein (the longest isoform of each Ensembl gene, and all Purdue proteins) was searched with DIAMOND (sensitive mode) against the animal, algal, bacterial and fungal proteins listed on the gene-transfer page. A protein was listed when its best overall match was not an animal protein, and was classed as a strong candidate when the bit score of that match exceeded that of the best non-sacoglossan animal match by at least 20%. In a separate analysis, 471 plastid and photosynthesis proteins were aligned to all four assemblies with miniprot. Overlapping hits were merged into loci, and the translation of each locus was searched against the same database to distinguish algal genes from the slug’s own homologues (scripts 21, 28 and 29).

11. Foreign contigs and junctions in the Purdue assembly

Contigs carrying algal genes outside the known chloroplast fragments were compared with the other assemblies (minimap2, asm20 preset), with two runs of xgElyCris1 reads, and with the plastid sequences of Dasycladales in GenBank (BLAST, dc-megablast). Junctions between chloroplast and nuclear DNA were tested with the Nanopore reads from the Purdue individual. Every read was mapped to a 4 kb bait sequence on the single-copy side of the junction (with repeats masked), and reads that reached the junction were aligned to a window around it. A read was counted as spanning the junction if a single alignment extended at least 300 bp beyond it on both sides, and as ending at the junction if its alignment ended within 150 bp of the junction with at least 200 bp of the read remaining unaligned (script 29).

Limitations

  • Gene functions are inferred from homology with Drosophila and mammals. No rhythms were measured.
  • The xgElyCris1.1 assembly is incomplete (BUSCO completeness 73.6%; about 60% of read k-mers are represented). As the catalogue shows, a gene that is missing from the assembly is not necessarily missing from the genome.
  • Six protein models are homology-guided translations based on a protein from another species, and may lack lineage-specific exons. These models are identified in the details of each gene.
  • Support values in the trees are FastTree local support values, not bootstrap values. Where a clade is weakly supported, the assignment relies on reciprocal best hits.
  • Expression values are from a single head sample collected at an unknown time of day.
  • This analysis was carried out independently and has not been reviewed by the authors of the genome.
