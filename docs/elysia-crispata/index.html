Circadian clock genes of Elysia crispata

The lettuce sea slug Elysia crispata retains chloroplasts from the algae it feeds on and keeps them photosynthetically active for months. This page lists the circadian clock genes identified in its genome. For each gene it gives the position in the Sanger Institute assembly xgElyCris1.1, whether the gene is complete in that assembly, and the protein it encodes.

Genes in the catalogue

The catalogue contains 47 genes. They include the two core activators (Clock and Bmal1), the repressors Period, Timeless and a mammalian-type Cryptochrome, a light-sensitive Drosophila-type Cryptochrome, and the components of the second feedback loop (Pdp1, Vrille, E75, Hr3 and Clockwork orange). There are also 15 kinases, phosphatases and other regulators of protein stability, seven output genes, nine opsins and four photolyases. Each gene was identified by reciprocal best hits and by its position in a phylogenetic tree of its gene family.

Status in the reference assembly

In xgElyCris1.1, 30 of the 47 genes are intact and four more are complete but contain a frameshift or stop codon in the assembly sequence. A further five are partial or fragmented (Bmal1, Timeout, Cwo, Nemo, Neuropsin), and eight are missing from the assembly (Pdp1, E75, Pp1α, Slimb, Pdfr, r-opsin 1, r-opsin 2, Xenopsin).

The missing genes are present in the genome

All 47 genes are complete in at least one of the other two E. crispata assemblies, and reads from the individual sequenced for xgElyCris1.1 cover their exons. The genes that are incomplete or missing in xgElyCris1.1 are therefore absent from the assembly, not from the genome. Several of these genes lie in regions with low read depth: Xenopsin (8 reads per exon), Nemo (9), E75 (10), Bmal1 (16) and Cwo (18), compared with a median of 67 for all clock genes. The sequencing library was amplified by PCR from a small amount of DNA, which results in uneven coverage of the genome.
