Algal source of the kleptoplasts in Elysia chlorotica

E. chlorotica is the best-known example of kleptoplasty: it feeds on the yellow-green alga Vaucheria litorea and retains its chloroplasts for months. Three read sets were analysed, from two adults and from eggs.

The kleptoplasts in both adults came from Vaucheria litorea. Of the plastid DNA, 99.6% in the long reads and 97.9% in the short reads matches the V. litorea plastid genome. The barcode gene rbcL, extracted from plastid contigs assembled from the short reads, is 100.0% identical to V. litorea records over 2,178 bases.

The plastid content is high. Plastid DNA makes up 3.52% of the sequenced bases in one adult and 11.0% in the other, which corresponds to about 177 and 601 copies of the plastid genome per copy of the slug nuclear genome.

The eggs contain almost no plastid DNA. In 22.4 Gb of egg DNA, only 13 read pairs match a plastid sequence of any kind, and two single reads match Vaucheria. Because eggs contain no ingested algal material, this library represents the germline alone. It shows what is inherited, but provides no information about events in the somatic cells of adults (see the gene-transfer page).

Every read was compared with a reference panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 complete plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and several distantly related plastid genomes to detect other matches), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned at base level to the panel and to the assembly of this species.

Identification of the alga

Reads and read pairs were assigned to the plastid reference that they match best. The long reads from this species are older, error-prone PacBio RS reads with an accuracy of about 92%, so their identity to the reference reflects the error rate of the reads and not the divergence of the alga. The short reads match with 100% identity.

The plastid read pairs from the short-read library were assembled into contigs with a total length of 110 kb (3 contigs), compared with 115 kb for the published plastid genome.

The plastid reads were assembled into contigs, and the contigs were grouped. A contig within 5% of a reference sequence is grouped with that reference. The remaining contigs have no close reference and are divided by read depth into those with at least one tenth of the depth of the first row and those with less. Depth is the mean read coverage of the contigs in a group, and indicates the relative abundance of each plastid genome in the animal. Only reads that resemble a reference sequence were assembled, so an alga without a close reference is represented only by its more conserved genes.

Each read is assigned to the reference sequence that it matches best. An identity close to 100% indicates that the reference sequence is from the same species as the alga on which the animal had fed. A substantially lower identity means only that the reference is the closest sequenced relative.

Algal nuclear and mitochondrial DNA

The panel contains one nuclear ribosomal DNA record of V. litorea that includes a variable spacer region. No long read matches it. Short reads cannot resolve this question, because they match algal ribosomal DNA records only in the conserved regions that are shared with the slug’s own genes.

Implications for studies of kleptoplast rhythms

Kleptoplast transcripts should be mapped to NC_011600.1, the plastid genome of Vaucheria litorea (115,341 bp, annotated). The short reads from the second adult match it with 100% identity.

When the slug assembly is used as a reference, the plastid fragments and bacterial sequences should be excluded (they are listed on the gene-transfer page). Otherwise, plastid transcripts will map to them and will appear to be transcripts of slug genes.

Limitations

  • The two adults are different individuals: a wild-caught animal (isolate EC2010, Martha’s Vineyard) for the long reads and an animal from a laboratory culture for the short reads. Their plastid contents differ (3.52% and 11.0% of the bases, respectively).
  • All six paired-end libraries from the second adult were analysed (102.5 Gb). Because so much of this DNA is chloroplast DNA, only one in 50 of the matching pairs was analysed in detail, and the plastid contigs were assembled from these pairs.
  • Both runs of the egg library were analysed (22.4 Gb).

Data for Elysia chlorotica: reads SRR7647856, SRR7647851, SRR7647852, SRR7647853, SRR7647854, SRR7647849, SRR7647850, SRR768897, SRR768899; assembly ElyChl2.0 (GCA_003991915.1). The methods and data sources are described on the methods page.
