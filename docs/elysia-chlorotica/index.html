Circadian clock genes of Elysia chlorotica

The 47 clock genes catalogued in E. crispata were searched for in ElyChl2.0, the scaffold-level assembly of Elysia chlorotica (557 Mb). For each gene, this page gives its position in the assembly, whether it is complete, and the protein it encodes. The sequenced specimen came from Martha’s Vineyard, Massachusetts, USA.

Genes found

Of the 47 genes, 39 are intact, and five more are complete but contain a frameshift or stop codon in the assembly sequence (Bmal1, Period, Cry-DASH 1, Vrille, Slimb). Three are partial or fragmented (Timeout, Lark, r-opsin 1).

Identification

Of the 47 gene models, 23 are annotated genes of this species. The other 24 were obtained by aligning the E. crispata protein to the genome, at loci where the annotation has no gene or only a shorter one. Each gene model was accepted only if it passed a reciprocal best-hit test against all E. crispata proteins, which distinguishes orthologues from paralogues. The proteins are 66 to 100% identical to their E. crispata orthologues (median 88%).

Limitations of the assembly

The assembly consists of 9,989 scaffolds built from short reads and early PacBio reads. Genes that appear partial here, or that have an interrupted reading frame, are more likely to reflect errors in the assembly than real changes in the genes.

A gene model is classed as intact when it covers at least 90% of the alignment positions that are present in most species and its coding sequence in the assembly contains no frameshift or stop codon. Models obtained by aligning the E. crispata protein may lack poorly conserved regions at the ends of a protein.

Genes searched for but not found

The seven clock-associated genes that were not found in E. crispata were also searched for in this genome, with the same Drosophila and mammalian query proteins. None of them was found.
