Algal source of the kleptoplasts in Elysia atroviridis

The individual sequenced for the genome was an adult collected at Qingdao, China, in 2024. The plastid DNA among its reads identifies the alga on which it had fed.

The kleptoplasts came from Bryopsis (a feather alga). A total of 68,891 reads are plastid DNA, and 99.97% of this DNA matches a single plastid genome with a median identity of 100.0%. This genome, PQ652170.1, is from China and was deposited as Bryopsis hypnoides.

Two closely related plastid types are present. Each barcode gene occurs as two sequences that differ by about 0.5%. One is found in about 94% of the reads and is identical to PQ652170.1. The other, found in about 6% of the reads, has a tufA sequence identical to GenBank records deposited as B. hypnoides and as B. plumosa. These genes do not clearly separate the species of this group, so the two types are best interpreted as two strains or sister species of Bryopsis.

The plastid content is high. Plastid DNA makes up 1.87% of all sequenced bases, which corresponds to about 113 copies of the plastid genome per copy of the slug nuclear genome. E. atroviridis is reported to retain functional chloroplasts for only a few days (Cruz et al. 2015), so this animal had probably fed shortly before it was sequenced.

Every read was compared with a reference panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 complete plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and several distantly related plastid genomes to detect other matches), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned at base level to the panel and to the assembly of this species.

Algal nuclear and mitochondrial DNA

No read matches algal nuclear ribosomal DNA across the variable spacer regions. In addition, only 14 reads match an algal mitochondrial genome, which corresponds to about one base of algal mitochondrial DNA per 4,559 bases of plastid DNA. As in E. crispata, the plastids had been retained but almost no other algal DNA.

Implications for studies of kleptoplast rhythms

Kleptoplast transcripts should be mapped to PQ652170.1 (Bryopsis hypnoides, 139,745 bp, annotated in GenBank). The main plastid type in this animal is identical to this genome in both barcode genes, and the minor type differs from it by about 0.5%.

E. atroviridis is reported to retain functional chloroplasts for only a few days, so the plastids in an animal derive from its most recent meals, and a rhythm observed in them may be a rhythm of recently ingested algal material.

Limitations

  • Bryopsis species are difficult to distinguish with rbcL and tufA, and GenBank contains records under several species names that are identical in these genes. The match to PQ652170.1 is exact, but the species name of that record is the one assigned by its submitters.
  • The results describe a single animal on the day it was collected.
  • The complete long-read run used for the assembly was analysed (30.6 Gb).

Data for Elysia atroviridis: reads SRR34916404; assembly ASM5905261v1 (GCA_059052615.1). The methods and data sources are described on the methods page.
