Comparison of clock genes across seven Elysia species

The 47 clock genes catalogued in E. crispata were searched for in every Elysia species with a genome assembly, and the encoded proteins were aligned and compared.

The set of clock genes is conserved across the genus. Of the 47 genes, 27 are complete in the reference assembly of all seven species. In each of the other species the two core activators (Clock and Bmal1), the repressors Period, Timeless and a mammalian-type Cryptochrome, and a light-sensitive Drosophila-type Cryptochrome are all present as complete genes.

Missing and partial genes mostly reflect the quality of the assemblies. The two assemblies built entirely or mainly from short reads have the largest numbers of genes that are not intact (14 in E. marginata and 8 in E. chlorotica), whereas the four assemblies built from high-accuracy long reads have 3 to 4 each. The exceptions are the Go-opsins, which vary in number between species, and r-opsin-like 1, for which the gene model is incomplete in E. crispata itself.

The proteins differ in their degree of conservation. The most conserved across the genus, by mean pairwise identity, are Ck2β (100%), Pp2a-C (100%) and Pp2a-B56ε (99%); the least conserved are Tipin (61%), Sirt1 (66%) and Vrille (70%). The values for the core loop are Cry2 (97%), Cry1 (87%), Clock (85%), Bmal1 (83%), Timeout (81%), Period (78%) and Timeless (77%).

A gene model is classed as intact when it covers at least 90% of the alignment positions that are present in most sequences and contains no frameshift or stop codon.

Species tree

The tree was inferred from a concatenated alignment of the 35 clock proteins that are complete in every species (21,190 aligned positions) and is rooted on the sea hare Aplysia californica.

Maximum-likelihood tree (IQ-TREE, LG+F+G4). Numbers are ultrafast bootstrap support values, and branch lengths are in substitutions per site. A tree based on clock proteins alone is intended as a guide to the relationships among the species on this site, not as a phylogeny of the genus.

Conservation of chromosomal linkage

Four of the assemblies are chromosome-level. Each has 15 chromosomes, which are numbered by size, so chromosome numbers do not correspond between species. Linkage can nevertheless be compared: of the 54 pairs of clock genes that lie on the same chromosome in E. crispata, 53 also lie on the same chromosome in all three other species.

Last updated 14 November 2026. All analyses are based on publicly available genome data. They were carried out independently of the groups that produced the genomes and have not been peer reviewed. Gene functions are inferred from homology with Drosophila and mammals; no rhythms were measured.
