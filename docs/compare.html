The same clock in seven sea slugs

The 47 clock genes of E. crispata, looked for in the genome of every Elysia species that has one, and their proteins laid side by side.

The clock is the same throughout the genus. 27 of the 47 genes are whole in the reference assembly of all seven species. Every one of the other species has both halves of the activator (Clock and Bmal1), the repressors Period, Timeless and a mammalian-type Cryptochrome, and a light-sensitive Cryptochrome, each as a whole gene.

Where a gene is missing or partial, the assembly is usually the reason. The two assemblies built wholly or mainly from short reads have the most genes that are not intact (Elysia marginata 14, Elysia chlorotica 8); the four built from accurate long reads have 3 to 4 each. Two genes are exceptions: the Go-opsins, whose number differs from species to species, and r-opsin-like 1, whose model is incomplete in E. crispata itself.

The proteins differ in how fast they change. The most alike across the genus are Ck2β 100%, Pp2a-C 100%, Pp2a-B56ε 99%; the least alike are Tipin 61%, Sirt1 66%, Vrille 70%. Among the core loop, Cry2 97%, Cry1 87%, Clock 85%, Bmal1 83%, Timeout 81%, Period 78%, Timeless 77%.

How the species are related

A tree from 35 clock proteins that are complete in every species, joined end to end (21,190 aligned positions), rooted on the sea hare Aplysia.

Maximum likelihood (IQ-TREE, LG+F+G4). Numbers are ultrafast bootstrap supports. Branch lengths are substitutions per site. A tree from clock proteins alone is a guide to the species on this site, not a phylogeny of the genus.

Do the genes stay on the same chromosomes?

Four of the assemblies are chromosome-level, each with 15 chromosomes numbered by size, so the numbers themselves do not correspond. What can be compared is company: of the 54 pairs of clock genes that share a chromosome in E. crispata, 53 share one in all three other species too.
