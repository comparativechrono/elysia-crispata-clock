The circadian clock in seven Elysia genomes

Sea slugs of the genus Elysia keep chloroplasts taken from the algae they eat, some for months. This site catalogues the clock genes in every Elysia genome that has been sequenced, names the algae whose chloroplasts each sequenced animal carried, and tests whether any algal DNA has entered the slug genome.

One clock, seven genomes

27 of the 47 clock genes are whole in the reference assembly of every species. Every species has the whole core loop: Clock, Bmal1, Period, Timeless and two Cryptochromes (in E. crispata, Bmal1 is whole in another assembly of the species, not the reference). The comparison page has the gene-by-species table, the protein alignments and a tree of the species.

What a gap means

The assemblies differ in quality far more than the animals differ in genes. A gene shown as partial or missing is, in most cases, a hole in that assembly. Each species page says what its assembly can and cannot show.

What is not here

Nothing on this site measures a rhythm. Gene roles are inferred from flies and mammals, and each genome comes from a single animal.
