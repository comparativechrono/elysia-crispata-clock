The lettuce sea slug’s clock, gene by gene

Elysia crispata keeps stolen chloroplasts photosynthesising for months. This is a catalogue of the circadian clock genes in its genome: where each one sits in the Sanger assembly xgElyCris1.1, whether the assembly has it whole, and the protein it encodes.

Select a gene to open it. The wiring is the textbook fly and mouse clock; which gene does what in Elysia has not been tested, and one RNA sample cannot show rhythm.

A complete clock, by homology

47 genes: both halves of the core activator (Clock and Bmal1), the repressors Period, Timeless and a mammalian-type Cryptochrome, a light-sensitive Cryptochrome, the second loop (Pdp1, Vrille, E75, Hr3, Clockwork orange), fifteen kinases, phosphatases and degradation factors, ten opsins and four photolyases. Each call rests on reciprocal best hits and a family tree.

The assembly misses some of it

30 genes are intact in xgElyCris1.1 and 4 more are whole but with a frameshift or other mismatch in the assembly sequence. 5 are partial or scrambled (Bmal1, Timeout, Cwo, Nemo, Neuropsin) and 8 are absent (Pdp1, E75, Pp1α, Slimb, Pdfr, r-opsin 1, r-opsin 2, Xenopsin).

Missing from the assembly, not the animal

All 47 genes are whole in at least one of the two other E. crispata assemblies, and reads from the sequenced animal itself cover their exons. Several of them sit in stretches the sequencing library barely sampled: Xenopsin (8 reads per exon), Nemo (9 reads per exon), E75 (10 reads per exon), Bmal1 (16 reads per exon), Cwo (18 reads per exon), against 67 for a typical clock gene. The library was amplified by PCR from very little DNA, which leaves holes like these.
