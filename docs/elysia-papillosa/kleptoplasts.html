Algal source of the kleptoplasts in Elysia papillosa

Two individuals from Tampa, Florida, were sequenced with PacBio HiFi reads in 2024: the one used for the assembly and a second animal. The plastid DNA among the reads of each identifies the alga on which it had fed.

The kleptoplasts came from a single alga, Penicillus lamourouxii (a shaving-brush alga). All 6,528 plastid reads from the assembled individual match one plastid genome, with a median identity of 100.0%. This genome, OZ494339.1, was assembled by the Sanger Institute from the same individual and deposited as P. lamourouxii. The barcode gene rbcL is present as a single sequence, and so is tufA, apart from 21 of 366 reads in which a run of eight adenines is one base shorter, a common type of sequencing error in homopolymers. There is no evidence of a second alga.

The second individual contained the same alga, but much less plastid DNA. It has 1,326 plastid reads, compared with 6,528 in the assembled individual, and plastid DNA makes up 0.014% of the sequenced bases, compared with 0.12%. Its rbcL and tufA sequences are identical to those of the first individual. In addition, 17 of its reads are plastid DNA of a flowering plant, 86 to 96% identical to the plastid genome of Arabidopsis; these may derive from seagrass ingested with its food.

The amount of plastid DNA is small. In the assembled individual, plastid DNA makes up 0.12% of the sequenced bases, which corresponds to about 14 copies of the plastid genome per copy of the slug nuclear genome. By comparison, the E. crispata individual had about 250.

Every read was compared with a reference panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 complete plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and several distantly related plastid genomes to detect other matches), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned at base level to the panel and to the assembly of this species.

Algal nuclear and mitochondrial DNA

Almost no algal nuclear DNA is present. Only 1 of the 5,406,003 reads from the assembled individual contains nuclear ribosomal DNA of a Penicillus species, and none of the reads from the second individual does. This read is about 99% identical to a P. pyriformis record across the variable spacer regions, where the slug’s own ribosomal DNA does not match at all. A trace of algal nuclear DNA was therefore present in the sample, most likely from food remaining in the gut. No read matches an algal mitochondrial genome.

This is relevant to the question of gene transfer. Because algal nuclear DNA is present, even in trace amounts, an algal gene found among the reads would not in itself show that the gene is part of the slug genome.

Implications for studies of kleptoplast rhythms

For animals with the same diet, kleptoplast transcripts should be mapped to OZ494339.1, the plastid genome assembled from the same individual (89,758 bp). This genome is not annotated in the archive. It is 99.4% identical, over nearly its whole length, to OZ372796.1, the second plastid genome from the E. crispata individual, for which gene coordinates derived by homology are available in the downloads for that species.

The algal source of the kleptoplasts changes with the diet of an animal. A transcriptome study should therefore confirm the identity of the kleptoplasts in its own animals, for which the two barcode genes are sufficient.

Limitations

  • The identification as P. lamourouxii is based on a single 562-base rbcL record in GenBank (DQ469333), to which the reads are identical, and on the name under which the Sanger Institute deposited the plastid genome. No tufA record is available for the species. The most similar species with full-length barcode sequences, P. dumetosus, is 97.8% (rbcL) and 98.5% (tufA) identical.
  • Both individuals are from the same locality. E. papillosa may also feed on other algae.
  • All three long-read runs of the project were analysed: 36.2 Gb from the assembled individual and 64.8 Gb from the second individual.

Data for Elysia papillosa: reads ERR14842698, ERR14842697, ERR14842696; assembly xgElyPapi4.2 (GCA_977015215.2). The methods and data sources are described on the methods page.
