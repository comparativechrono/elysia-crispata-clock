Has algal DNA entered this slug’s genome?

Two places to look: the assembly, and the reads. For this species the reads of two animals are public, the one that was assembled and a second one.

No sign of it. Nothing in the assembly joins algal DNA to slug DNA, and in the reads of two animals no molecule holds chloroplast DNA inside slug DNA.

In the assembly: there is no plastid DNA at all. One contig of 31 kb is a piece of a green-algal mitochondrial genome, most likely that of the alga in the animal’s gut. It stands alone and carries no slug gene.

In the reads: in the assembled animal 7 of 6,533 chloroplast reads are joined to slug DNA at one end, and 4 of 2,012 reads of the slug’s own mitochondrial DNA; in the second animal, 4 of 1,326 and 31 of 7,954. Chloroplast DNA is joined to slug DNA no more often than mitochondrial DNA is. Had the cells carried one insertion in every 5 cells in the first animal, or one insertion in every 8 cells in the second, about three insertion-shaped reads would be expected. There are none.

In the assembly

The assembly was cut into 10 kb windows and each window compared with the same panel as the reads. 10,595 matches were set aside because they fall on stretches of the panel that are hit from 15 or more places in the assembly: slug repeats that happen to resemble a short piece of a panel sequence. 31 more were to low-complexity sequence, and 9 were the slug’s own ribosomal DNA, which resembles algal ribosomal DNA at 80 to 90%.

No sequence of the assembly is plastid DNA, as a whole or in part. The one match kept is 425 bp that resemble a mitochondrial ribosomal RNA gene of the green alga Caulerpa. It lies on the contig that the protein screen below identifies as algal mitochondrial DNA.

In the reads

A slug takes chloroplasts into cells of its gut, not into its eggs or sperm. If a piece of chloroplast DNA were built into a chromosome, that would happen in one body cell, and only that cell and its daughters would carry it. In DNA from a whole animal such an insertion is carried by a few molecules, perhaps by one, and the other molecules from the same place do not have it. So a single read that joins chloroplast DNA to slug DNA is all the evidence there can be, and reads that disagree with it are expected.

The difficulty is that preparing DNA for sequencing also joins unrelated molecules to each other now and then, and in a single read such a join looks the same. Three things can be asked of the reads.

  • Shape. A fragment that sits in a chromosome has slug DNA on both sides, and the two sides are neighbours in the genome. A join made in the tube puts two molecules end to end. A read that goes slug DNA, organelle DNA, and then the same stretch of slug DNA carrying on is called an insertion shape here. Two accidental joins that happen to pick neighbours do not occur by chance, so this shape needs another explanation.
  • Rate. Joining in the tube treats all DNA alike. Chloroplast DNA should then be joined to slug DNA as often as mitochondrial DNA is, and as often as one piece of slug DNA is joined to an unrelated one. Real insertions would come on top of that.
  • The same join twice. A join found in two molecules that are not copies of each other is no accident of the library. It means a clone of cells, or an insertion that was inherited. An insertion in a single cell cannot pass this test and does not have to.

What this library could show: reads long enough to hold a 500-base insertion with 300 bases of slug DNA on each side cover each place in the genome 29.3 times. Each read comes from one cell, so in such reads the library holds about 15 cells’ worth of DNA. An insertion carried by every cell on one of its two chromosome copies would be met by about that many reads, and insertions scattered over different cells add up in the same way: with one insertion in every 5 cells, averaged over all the cells that went into the tube, about three insertion-shaped reads would be expected.

Both long-read runs of the assembled animal were read in full. The library was not amplified.

Single joins. 7 of 6,533 chloroplast reads are joined to slug DNA at one end (6 of them to slug DNA that can be placed), and so are 4 of 2,012 reads of the slug’s own mitochondrial DNA. Each read holds two pieces and no more, the organelle piece running to the end of the read. Mitochondrial DNA is joined to slug DNA at least as often as chloroplast DNA is, which is what joining in the tube gives and an insertion of chloroplast DNA does not. All of them are in the table of joins for download.

The comparison with slug DNA says little here: pieces of slug DNA go on with slug DNA from elsewhere far more often (2.85%), nearly all of it real difference between this animal’s chromosomes and an assembly that is still in contigs.

Insertion shapes. None, for chloroplast or for mitochondrial DNA. The search went down to pieces of 100 bases lying inside a read that otherwise follows the slug sequence.

The second animal, a whole adult from the same place, was sequenced more deeply, from a library that had been amplified by PCR. It held about a tenth as much chloroplast DNA as the first (0.014% of the bases). Its reads were placed on the assembly of the first animal.

Single joins. 4 of 1,326 chloroplast reads are joined to slug DNA at one end, and 31 of 7,954 mitochondrial reads. The amplification adapter lies between the two pieces in all 4 chloroplast joins and in 29 of the 31 mitochondrial ones: these were made in the tube.

Insertion shapes. None. 29 reads do carry an extra stretch that the first pass had matched to a plastid genome by a few seeds, 28 of them at one place. Base by base it is not plastid DNA: this animal has three more copies of a 45-base repeat there than the assembled one, and the repeat shares one seed with a repeat in the plastid genome of an unrelated alga.

Algal genes, searched as proteins

DNA comparison can miss a gene from an alga that has no close relative among the references. So 471 proteins were aligned straight to the assembly: the plastid-encoded proteins of a green alga, photosynthesis proteins of Chlamydomonas and Arabidopsis (including those encoded in the nucleus), proteins of siphonous green algae, and every available protein of Vaucheria litorea. Each place that matched was translated and compared with animal, algal and bacterial proteins.

176 places matched. Of these, 97 are the slug’s own genes: a protein of another animal is the best match, as when mitochondrial ATP synthase is found with the chloroplast one. Another 78 match no protein of any other organism: 70 are low-complexity alignments and the rest are short or weak ones (25 to 40% identity, stitched together across several exons) whose translation matches nothing.

Each sequence that carries a place of another kind was then translated as a whole, in all six frames, and every protein-coding stretch found on it was compared with the same proteins. The table gives their number, how many are of bacterial type, and the share of the sequence they take up. Bacterial DNA is mostly coding; a slug sequence of this size would hold a gene or two. GC is the share of G and C among the bases; the genome as a whole has 38%.

The one place where the best match is an algal protein is on contig CDRNNR020002991.1 (30.9 kb). Translated in all frames, that contig carries a row of mitochondrial genes (nad5, cox2, nad3, atpA, nad4) whose best matches are mitochondrial proteins of green algae, and atpA is split by introns. It is a piece of an algal mitochondrial genome, assembled as its own contig. Anyone using the assembly as slug sequence should set it aside.

What this cannot rule out

  • The limits given here are averages over every cell that went into the tube. Chloroplasts are held only in cells of the digestive gland. If those were a tenth of the animal’s cells, the limit for them alone is ten times weaker.
  • The read test recognises plastid DNA of the alga these animals carried and of the other algae in the panel. The protein screen finds only genes with a relative among the 471 proteins searched.
  • The assembly is still in contigs. Sequence that is missing from it cannot be searched.
