Algal source of the kleptoplasts in Elysia leucolegnote

No reads from the individual used for the assembly are publicly available. Two other individuals of the species, one from Shenzhen and one from Hainan, have been sequenced with short reads, and their plastid DNA was analysed instead.

The main source of the kleptoplasts in both individuals is a species of Boodleopsis. Of the plastid DNA that could be identified, 92.3% in the Shenzhen individual and 68.7% in the Hainan individual matches the plastid genome of a Boodleopsis species. Contigs assembled from these reads are 97 to 99% identical to that genome. Boodleopsis, a small green alga that grows on mangrove mud, is the reported food of this slug (Swennen 2011).

The alga is not the Boodleopsis species represented by a barcode sequence in GenBank. The rbcL gene of the main alga is 87.3% identical to the B. pusilla record in GenBank. The plastid genome that it matches was deposited as Boodleopsis sp., without a species name. The plastid genomes from the two individuals are 99.5% identical to each other over 71 kb.

Other algae are also present. In the Shenzhen individual, the plastid contigs fall into groups with different read depths (see the table below). The Boodleopsis genome has a read depth of about 404×. Nine contigs that do not closely match any reference (81 to 89% identical to the closest) have a depth of about 103×. They represent a second alga with no close relative in GenBank, about one quarter as abundant as the first. The fragment of plastid DNA found in the assembly (see the gene-transfer page) has a depth of 102× in this individual, similar to the contigs of that alga. A further 41 contigs are within 5% of another Boodleopsis plastid genome in GenBank, that of isolate FL1161, and have a depth of about 7×; they represent a second Boodleopsis species, present in small amounts.

The other algae are more abundant in the Hainan individual. Its Boodleopsis genome has a read depth of about 191×. The alga without a reference is also present, with a sequence identical to that in the Shenzhen individual and a depth of 75 to 124×. A third plastid genome, which is 86 to 89% identical to the second in the same genes and no more similar to any reference, has a depth of 150 to 210×, and is thus as abundant as the Boodleopsis genome. In this individual, therefore, two algae that cannot be identified contributed more plastid DNA than the one that can.

Identified plastid DNA makes up 0.28% of the sequenced bases in the Shenzhen individual and 0.074% in the Hainan individual. A read is counted as plastid DNA only if it resembles a reference sequence, so algae without a close reference are under-represented in these values and in the proportions given above. Both values are therefore minimum estimates.

Every read was compared with a reference panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 complete plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and several distantly related plastid genomes to detect other matches), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned at base level to the panel and to the assembly of this species.

Identification of the alga

Because these are short reads, a single read cannot span a barcode gene. Each read pair was assigned to the plastid reference that it matches best, and the plastid read pairs were assembled into contigs, from which the barcode genes were extracted. The reads from the Shenzhen individual yielded 83 contigs (with a total length of 176 kb, the longest being 69.7 kb), and those from the Hainan individual yielded 96 contigs (210 kb).

Algal nuclear and mitochondrial DNA

Short reads cannot show whether algal nuclear DNA is present. The read pairs that match algal ribosomal DNA records do so only in the most conserved regions of the genes, and they match unrelated green algae such as Ulva equally well. These reads derive from the slug’s own ribosomal DNA.

Implications for studies of kleptoplast rhythms

The closest reference for mapping kleptoplast transcripts is NC_039521.1 (Boodleopsis sp., 75,962 bp, annotated in GenBank), which is 97 to 99% identical to the main plastid type in the two individuals analysed. The plastid contigs from both individuals are available in the downloads.

Transcripts from the other algae present in these animals will map poorly, or not at all, to this reference. A proportion of unmapped plastid-like reads is therefore to be expected, and should not be regarded as noise.

Limitations

  • Neither individual is the one whose genome was assembled (isolate GZ-2025, Dongzhai Harbor, Hainan). The kleptoplasts of that individual are unknown, apart from the single fragment of plastid DNA in its assembly.
  • The identification is based on a plastid genome that was deposited without a species name. The match is close but not exact, so the alga may be a different species of the same genus.
  • The proportions refer to plastid DNA that resembles a reference sequence in the panel. Algae without a close reference are under-represented.
  • Both libraries were analysed in full: 12.5 Gb from the Shenzhen individual and 25.5 Gb from the Hainan individual.
