Whose chloroplasts was this slug carrying?

No reads of the animal behind the assembly are public. Two other animals of the species have been sequenced with short reads, one from Shenzhen and one from Hainan, and their plastid DNA was examined instead.

A Boodleopsis, in both animals. 92.3% of the plastid DNA that could be recognised in the Shenzhen animal and 68.7% in the Hainan animal matches the plastid genome of a Boodleopsis species. Contigs assembled from those reads are 97 to 99% identical to it. Boodleopsis, a tiny green alga of mangrove mud, is the food reported for this slug (Swennen 2011).

Not the Boodleopsis with a barcode in GenBank. The rbcL gene of the main alga is 87.3% identical to the B. pusilla record in GenBank. The plastid genome it does match is filed as Boodleopsis sp. without a species name. The plastid genomes of the two animals are 99.5% identical to each other over 71 kb.

Other algae too. In the Shenzhen animal the plastid contigs fall into kinds by how deeply the reads cover them (table below). The Boodleopsis genome is covered about 404 times. Nine contigs that match no reference closely (81 to 89% identical to the nearest) are covered about 103 times: a second alga with no close relative in GenBank, about a quarter as plentiful as the first. The piece of plastid DNA that turned up in the assembly (see the gene transfer page) is covered 102 times in this animal, as that alga’s contigs are. 41 more contigs lie within 5% of another Boodleopsis plastid genome in GenBank, that of isolate FL1161, and are covered about 7 times: a second Boodleopsis, in small amounts.

More of them in the Hainan animal. Its Boodleopsis genome is covered about 191 times. The alga without a reference is there too, identical in sequence to that of the Shenzhen animal and covered 75 to 124 times. A third plastid genome, 86 to 89% identical to that one in the same genes and no closer to any reference, is covered 150 to 210 times: as plentiful as the Boodleopsis. So in this animal two algae that cannot be named held more plastid DNA than the one that can.

Recognised plastid DNA is 0.28% of the sequenced bases in the Shenzhen animal and 0.074% in the Hainan animal. A read is counted as plastid DNA only if it resembles a reference, so algae without a close reference count for less in these figures and in the shares than they should: both figures are floors.

Which alga

These are short reads, so a single read cannot span a barcode gene. Each read pair was assigned to the plastid reference it matches best, and the plastid pairs were assembled into contigs from which the barcode genes were cut. The Shenzhen animal gives 83 contigs (176 kb in all, the longest 69.7 kb), the Hainan animal 96 (210 kb).

Did algal nuclei come along?

Short reads cannot say. Read pairs that match algal ribosomal DNA records do so only in the most conserved parts of the genes, and match unrelated green algae such as Ulva as well as anything else. They are the slug’s own ribosomal DNA.

For rhythm work

The nearest reference is NC_039521.1 (Boodleopsis sp., 75,962 bp, annotated in GenBank), 97 to 99% identical to the main plastid type of the two animals examined. The plastid contigs of both animals are in the downloads.

Transcripts from the other algae these animals carried will map poorly or not at all to that reference. A share of plastid-like reads that will not map is to be expected, and is not noise.

Downloads: plastid contigs (Shenzhen animal), barcode sequences (Shenzhen animal), plastid contigs (Hainan animal), barcode sequences (Hainan animal), and all the numbers on this page.

What to keep in mind

  • Neither animal is the one whose genome was assembled (isolate GZ-2025, Dongzhai Harbor, Hainan). What that animal carried is not known, apart from the one piece of plastid DNA in its assembly.
  • The name rests on a plastid genome filed without a species name. The match is close but not exact, so the alga eaten may be a different species of the same genus.
  • Shares are of the plastid DNA that resembles a reference in the panel. Algae with no close reference count for less than they should.
  • Both libraries were examined in full: 12.5 Gb from the Shenzhen animal and 25.5 Gb from the Hainan animal.
