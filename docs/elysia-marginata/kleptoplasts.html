Algal source of the kleptoplasts in Elysia marginata

The genome was assembled from a single individual collected at Kagoshima, Japan, in 2013. The plastid DNA among its reads identifies the alga on which it had fed.

The kleptoplasts came from Bryopsis (a feather alga). Of the plastid DNA, 99.5% matches a single plastid genome with a median identity of 100.0%. This genome, PQ652170.1, is from China and was deposited as Bryopsis hypnoides.

The plastid type is the same as in the E. atroviridis individual. Both barcode genes, extracted from plastid contigs assembled from these reads, are identical to that plastid genome and to the main plastid type in the E. atroviridis individual from Qingdao (over 743 bases of rbcL and 1,228 bases of tufA).

The plastid content is moderate. Plastid DNA makes up 0.26% of the sequenced bases, which corresponds to about 15 copies of the plastid genome per copy of the slug nuclear genome.

Every read was compared with a reference panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 complete plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and several distantly related plastid genomes to detect other matches), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned at base level to the panel and to the assembly of this species.

Identification of the alga

Because these are short reads, a single read cannot span a barcode gene. Each read pair was therefore assigned to the plastid reference that it matches best, and the plastid read pairs were assembled into contigs (3 contigs with a total length of 136 kb, the longest being 102.5 kb), from which the barcode genes were extracted.

The plastid reads were assembled into contigs, and the contigs were grouped. A contig within 5% of a reference sequence is grouped with that reference. The remaining contigs have no close reference and are divided by read depth into those with at least one tenth of the depth of the first row and those with less. Depth is the mean read coverage of the contigs in a group, and indicates the relative abundance of each plastid genome in the animal. Only reads that resemble a reference sequence were assembled, so an alga without a close reference is represented only by its more conserved genes.

Each read is assigned to the reference sequence that it matches best. An identity close to 100% indicates that the reference sequence is from the same species as the alga on which the animal had fed. A substantially lower identity means only that the reference is the closest sequenced relative.

Algal nuclear and mitochondrial DNA

Algal mitochondrial DNA is present. A total of 4,169 read pairs match the mitochondrial genome of Bryopsis hypnoides with 99.8% identity, which corresponds to about one base of algal mitochondrial DNA per 188 bases of plastid DNA.

Short reads cannot show whether algal nuclear DNA is present. The read pairs that match algal ribosomal DNA records do so only in the most conserved regions of the genes, and they match unrelated green algae such as Ulva equally well. These reads derive from the slug’s own ribosomal DNA.

Implications for studies of kleptoplast rhythms

Kleptoplast transcripts should be mapped to PQ652170.1 (Bryopsis hypnoides, 139,745 bp, annotated in GenBank), to which the plastid DNA of this animal is identical in both barcode genes. The plastid contigs assembled from its reads (3 contigs, 136 kb) are available in the downloads; they include a 3.7 kb region that is absent from the reference.

The reads are deposited under the name Elysia ornata. Other individuals of the species may contain kleptoplasts from other Bryopsis species, which can be checked with the two barcode genes.

Limitations

  • Bryopsis species are difficult to distinguish with rbcL and tufA. The match to PQ652170.1 is exact, but the species name of that record is the one assigned by its submitters.
  • Both short-insert libraries used for the assembly were analysed in full (84.4 Gb).
  • The reads are deposited in the archive under the name Elysia ornata.

Data for Elysia marginata: reads DRR238951, DRR238952; assembly Ema_v1 (GCA_019649035.1). The methods and data sources are described on the methods page.
