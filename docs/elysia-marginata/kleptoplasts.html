Whose chloroplasts was this slug carrying?

The genome was assembled from one animal collected at Kagoshima, Japan, in 2013. The plastid DNA among its reads says which alga it had been eating.

A feather alga, Bryopsis. 99.6% of the plastid DNA matches one plastid genome at a median identity of 100.0%: PQ652170.1, from China, filed as Bryopsis hypnoides.

The same kind as in the E. atroviridis animal. Both barcode genes, cut from plastid contigs assembled out of these reads, are identical to that plastid genome and to the main type in the E. atroviridis animal from Qingdao (rbcL over 743 bases, tufA over 1,228).

A modest amount. Plastid DNA is 0.27% of the sequenced bases, about 15 copies of the plastid genome for every copy of the slug genome.

Every read was compared with a panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 whole plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and distant plastids to catch anything else), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned base by base to the panel and to this species’ own assembly.

Which alga

These are short reads, so a single read cannot span a barcode gene. Instead, each read pair was assigned to the plastid reference it matches best, and the plastid pairs were assembled into contigs (4 contigs, 136 kb in all, the longest 101.4 kb) from which the barcode genes were cut.

Did algal nuclei come along?

Algal mitochondria did. 235 read pairs match the mitochondrial genome of Bryopsis hypnoides at 99.8%, about 1 base for every 183 of plastid DNA.

For algal nuclei, short reads cannot say. Read pairs that match algal ribosomal DNA records do so only in the most conserved parts of the genes, and match unrelated green algae such as Ulva as well as anything else. They are the slug’s own ribosomal DNA.

For rhythm work

The plastid contigs assembled from its reads (4 contigs, 136 kb) are in the downloads; they include a 3.7 kb stretch that the reference lacks.

The reads are filed under the name Elysia ornata, and other animals of the species may carry other Bryopsis. The two barcode genes are enough to check.

What to keep in mind

  • Bryopsis species are hard to tell apart with rbcL and tufA. The match to PQ652170.1 is exact; the species name on that record is the submitters’.
  • 4.5 Gb were examined: the first part of one of the two short-insert libraries behind the assembly (37.6 Gb).
  • The reads are filed in the archive under the name Elysia ornata.
