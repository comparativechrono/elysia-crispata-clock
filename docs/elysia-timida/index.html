What was found

44 of the 47 genes are intact and one more is whole but needs a frameshift or stop codon to read through (Timeout). One is partial or in pieces (r-opsin-like 1), and one has no counterpart (Go-opsin 1).

How the genes were found

The assembly has no public gene annotation, so all 46 models are the E. crispata protein aligned to the genome. Every model had to pass a reciprocal test against all E. crispata proteins, which keeps paralogues apart. The proteins are 59 to 100% identical to their E. crispata counterparts (median 87%).

What to keep in mind

A chromosome-level assembly. Its gene annotation is not in GenBank, so every model here is the E. crispata protein aligned to the genome.

A model is called intact when it fills at least 90% of the alignment positions that most species share and the genome reads through without a frameshift or stop. Models aligned from E. crispata can miss poorly conserved ends of a protein.

Looked for and not found

The seven clock-associated genes that E. crispata lacks were searched for here too, with the same fly and mammal proteins. None has a counterpart in this genome either.
