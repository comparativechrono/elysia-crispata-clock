Algal source of the kleptoplasts in Elysia timida

The genome was assembled from adults collected at Cadaqués, Spain. E. timida is known to feed on Acetabularia acetabulum (mermaid’s wineglass). The reads are consistent with this, as far as the available reference sequences allow.

The kleptoplasts came from Acetabularia acetabulum. Of the plastid DNA that could be identified, 87.5% matches GenBank records of this alga, with a median identity of 99.7%. The remainder matches plastid genes of other algae of the same order with about 90% identity; these are Acetabularia genes for which no A. acetabulum sequence is available in GenBank.

Most of the plastid DNA could not be identified. No complete plastid genome of Acetabularia has been published. GenBank contains 51 fragments with a total length of 49 kb, which is only part of the genome. Only reads that overlap one of these fragments, or a similar gene of a related alga, were counted, so the value of 0.005% of sequenced bases is a minimum.

Nevertheless, the plastid content is low. The known fragments of the plastid genome have a read depth of about 58×, compared with 70× for the slug nuclear genome. In the E. crispata individual, the read depth of the plastid genome was about 250 times that of the nuclear genome. Part of this difference may be due to the way in which the library was prepared (see below).

Every read was compared with a reference panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 complete plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and several distantly related plastid genomes to detect other matches), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned at base level to the panel and to the assembly of this species.

Identification of the alga

Each read was assigned to the plastid reference that it matches best. In the complete run, both barcode genes are spanned by reads: tufA by 55 reads, which all have the same sequence and are identical to the A. acetabulum record over 1,230 bases, and rbcL by 28 reads, which all have the same sequence and are 99.6% identical to the A. acetabulum record.

Algal nuclear and mitochondrial DNA

No read matches algal nuclear ribosomal DNA across the variable spacer regions, and none matches an algal mitochondrial genome.

Implications for studies of kleptoplast rhythms

No complete Acetabularia plastid genome is available as a reference for mapping transcripts. GenBank contains 51 fragments of the A. acetabulum plastid genome, with a total length of 49 kb.

A larger set of sequences is available from another source. One assembly of E. crispata (the Purdue assembly, GCA_033675545.1) contains 646 kb of Acetabularia plastid DNA as separate contigs, derived from an animal that had fed on that alga (see the gene-transfer page for E. crispata). These contigs are from a different species of Acetabularia: for the genes that can be compared, they are 90 to 96% identical to those of A. acetabulum. They are therefore useful as a guide to the gene content of the genome, but not as a reference for quantifying reads.

Limitations

  • For the reason given above, the proportion of plastid DNA is a minimum. A complete Acetabularia plastid genome would be needed to measure it.
  • The library was amplified by PCR from a very small amount of DNA, which distorts the proportions of the sequenced DNA.
  • The complete run used for the assembly was analysed (53.1 Gb).

Data for Elysia timida: reads SRR30016879; assembly ASM4364404v1 (GCA_043644045.1). The methods and data sources are described on the methods page.
