Whose chloroplasts was this slug carrying?

The genome was assembled from adults collected at Cadaqués, Spain. E. timida is known to feed on the mermaid’s wineglass, Acetabularia acetabulum. The reads agree, as far as they can be read.

Acetabularia acetabulum. 87.5% of the plastid DNA that could be recognised matches GenBank records of that alga at a median identity of 99.7%. The rest matches plastid genes of other algae of the same order at around 90%: these are Acetabularia genes for which GenBank has no A. acetabulum sequence.

Most of the plastid DNA could not be recognised. No complete plastid genome of Acetabularia has been published. GenBank holds 51 pieces of it, 49 kb in all, which is only part of the genome. Only reads that touch one of those pieces, or a similar gene of a related alga, count here, so the 0.005% of sequenced bases is a floor.

Still, the load is light. The known pieces of the plastid genome are covered about 58 times over, against 70 times for the slug’s own genome. In the E. crispata animal the plastid genome was covered roughly 250 times more deeply than the slug’s. Part of the difference may come from how the library was made (see below).

Every read was compared with a panel of 1,271 algal plastid sequences (241 whole plastid genomes of green algae, plastid records of Dasycladales, Xanthophyceae and Cladophorales, and distant plastids to catch anything else), 117 algal mitochondrial genomes, 7,352 nuclear ribosomal DNA sequences of algae and the mitochondrial genomes of nine Elysia species. Reads with a match were then aligned base by base to the panel and to this species’ own assembly.

Did algal nuclei come along?

No read matches algal nuclear ribosomal DNA across its variable spacers, and none matches an algal mitochondrial genome.

For rhythm work

There is no complete Acetabularia plastid genome to map transcripts to. GenBank holds 51 pieces of the A. acetabulum plastid genome, 49 kb in all.

A larger resource exists by accident. One assembly of E. crispata (the Purdue assembly, GCA_033675545.1) contains 646 kb of Acetabularia plastid DNA as contigs of its own, left from an animal that had eaten that alga (see the gene transfer page of E. crispata). It comes from another species of Acetabularia: where the genes can be compared, they are 90 to 96% identical to those of A. acetabulum. So it is a guide to what the genome holds, and not a reference to count reads against.

Downloads: barcode sequences, and all the numbers on this page.

What to keep in mind

  • The plastid share is a minimum, for the reason given above. A complete Acetabularia plastid genome would be needed to measure it.
  • The library was amplified by PCR from very little DNA, which distorts the proportions of what was sequenced.
  • The whole run behind the assembly was examined (53.1 Gb).
